The Monk Diamond

The Stone

The Monk Diamond is among the rarest jewels ever recorded — a vivid green stone over one billion years old, valued at more than ten million dollars. Found in 1880, it was purchased nine years later by a Russian nobleman as a gift for his wife, and entered the highest circles of imperial society.

Professor Bairstone and Dr Day, on a fossil-hunting expedition in Siberia, made an extraordinary discovery: the Monk Diamond, concealed inside a remote cave — bringing to a close one of history's most enduring gemological mysteries.

A Turbulent History

1880

The diamond is discovered in a remote cave in Australia. The finding team describes their discovery as one for the ages.

1889

A Russian nobleman purchases the stone as a gift for his wife, bringing it to St Petersburg where it becomes the jewel of the imperial social scene.

1917

During the Russian Revolution, the diamond is stolen from the nobleman's palace in St Petersburg. Its whereabouts become unknown.

1947

In a police raid in Moscow, the diamond is recovered from a gang of petty criminals. It is returned to the nobleman's surviving son.

1950s

Believing the stone to be cursed, the nobleman's son sells it for an extraordinary sum. The House of Volkov — prominent jewel salesmen — becomes the new owner and places it on public display.

Recent

Three years ago, the Bond Brothers carry out a daring theft from the House of Volkov. The diamond vanishes once more — until its dramatic recovery in Siberia.
